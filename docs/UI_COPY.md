## Find your way

Use Orbit to circle the statue, or Wander to move through the desert. Select a plus beside the ruin to read its lines.

- Drag in Orbit: Look around the scene
- Drag in Wander: Move forward, back, and sideways
- Alt + drag in Wander: Look around
- Right-click and drag, or Shift + drag: Pan across the scene
- Two fingers: Pan on a touch screen
- Scroll: Zoom
- Pinch: Zoom in Orbit
- WASD: Move in Wander
- Hold Shift: Move faster in Wander
- Arrow keys: Look up, down, and around
- Shift + arrows: Pan sideways or vertically
- Space or Page Up: Rise
- C, Shift + Space or Page Down: Lower your viewpoint
- + and −: Zoom
- Tab, then Enter: Read a focused marker
- Escape: Close the poem or a menu

You can travel up to 160 meters from the statue and rise 75 meters above the desert.

## About the work

Explore the ruined statue in Percy Bysshe Shelley’s Ozymandias, or choose Enter the poem for a guided reading through the desert.

The tour moves through all fourteen lines automatically. You can pause, go back, or skip ahead whenever you like. Select a plus beside the statue to read its lines, or select a line in the poem to visit that part of the ruin.

The poem keeps its original wording, punctuation, line breaks, and alignment. Turn motion off to stop the blowing sand and camera transitions. You can still explore with the movement controls. Sound is optional.

### Text and materials

The poem follows the Academy of American Poets edition. This world is a literary interpretation of the poem, rather than a historical reconstruction.

The terrain and ruins were built for this world. The legs and hand are modeled from MakeHuman Community’s CC0 base mesh. The fallen head uses Daniel Pett’s scan of the Ramesses II colossus at Memphis, licensed under CC BY 4.0. It was cropped, sealed, and reshaped to bring out the expression Shelley describes.

The sand and stone textures come from Poly Haven under CC0. Charlotte Baglioni created the sand material. Dimitrios Savva photographed the stone, and Rico Cilliers processed it.

EB Garamond by Georg Duffner and Octavio Pardo sets all interface and poem text. It’s licensed under the SIL Open Font License. Cinzel by Natanael Gama remains carved into the pedestal.

The optional sound uses a gentle air recording by dhallcomposer under CC0. It’s filtered and mixed softly, with long overlapping passages.
